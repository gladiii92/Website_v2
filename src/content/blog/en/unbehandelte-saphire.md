## The Fascination of Genuine, Untreated Sapphires

Sapphires are undoubtedly among the most sought-after and precious gemstones in the world. Their deep, mesmerizing color and extreme hardness (second only to diamonds) have made them symbols of power, wisdom, and eternal love for millennia. However, the reality of the global gemstone market today is different from what many buyers assume: **Nearly 90% to 95% of all sapphires on the market are heated** to artificially enhance their color and dissolve small inclusions.

A completely untreated, "unheated" sapphire is therefore an extremely rare wonder of nature. Such a stone reflects the pure magic of the earth, exactly as it was formed in the depths of the rock over millions of years—without any human intervention.

### What Does "Heating" Mean for Gemstones?

Heating sapphires is a practice that has been known in part since antiquity. In modern ovens, rough stones are often heated to temperatures well over 1,000 degrees Celsius, sometimes up to 1,800 degrees. This heat treatment causes two significant changes in the stone:
1. **Color Enhancement:** Pale stones can develop a deep blue, or undesirable color tints are eliminated.
2. **Clarity:** So-called "silk" inclusions (rutile needles) are melted by the heat and integrated into the crystal lattice, making the stone appear more transparent and pure.

It is important to emphasize that simple heating (only by heat, without adding chemicals like beryllium) is **generally accepted** in the jewelry industry. It makes the stones visually appealing and affordable for the broader market. However, it massively reduces their rarity value. For collectors, investors, and lovers of true nature, heated stones are usually of lesser interest.

### Why Are Untreated Sapphires So Valuable?

The main reason for the enormous value of untreated sapphires is their incredible rarity. When nature creates a stone that already possesses perfect color, outstanding transparency, and excellent fire without any manipulation, it is a geological "lottery win."

Furthermore, untreated stones are considered highly stable in value and have seen consistent appreciation over recent decades. Particularly origins like **Kashmir**, **Burma (Myanmar)**, or specific mines in **Sri Lanka (Ceylon)** and **Madagascar** produce highly coveted, untreated sapphires.

### How to Recognize Untreated Sapphires

To the untrained eye, it is almost impossible to distinguish a high-quality heated sapphire from an untreated one. Even experienced gemologists rely on high-tech equipment. Here are the most important points to look out for:

#### 1. The Gemological Certificate (The Most Important Point!)
Never rely solely on a dealer's statement. A high-quality, untreated sapphire must **always** be certified by an independent, internationally recognized laboratory.
Look for certificates from:
- **GIA** (Gemological Institute of America)
- **SSEF** (Swiss Gemmological Institute)
- **Gübelin Gem Lab**
- **Lotus Gemology**
- **IGI** or **DSEF**

Look in the certificate for the **"Treatment"** section. It must explicitly state: **"No indications of heating"**.

#### 2. Microscopic Inclusions ("Silk")
Nature almost always leaves its fingerprints. While completely flawless, unheated sapphires do exist, they are so rare that they are almost priceless. Under a microscope (or a good loupe), fine, intersecting rutile needles can often be seen in untreated sapphires—the so-called "silk." In heated stones, this silk is mostly partially or completely melted, leaving fluid or "burst" crystalline structures.

#### 3. Color Zoning
Many natural sapphires exhibit slight color zoning. This means that the color is not distributed 100% evenly throughout the stone but runs in slight bands. While heating is often used to make these zonings disappear, in untreated stones, it is an indicator of their natural origin. A master cutter will facet the stone so that this zoning is barely visible from above through the table facet play.

#### 4. The Price Indicator
Quality and rarity come at a price. If you are offered a seemingly perfect, deep blue, large sapphire at a price that is "too good to be true," then it usually is. In such cases, these are almost always heavily heated (sometimes beryllium diffusion-treated) or even synthetic (lab-grown) stones.
